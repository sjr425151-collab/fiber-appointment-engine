# Phase 1 — Brand strategy

## Positioning

**For** households tired of bills that creep up, **TUCKER is** the home-service savings broker **that** compares internet, mobile, TV, energy and more in one place and helps you switch only if it's worth it. **Unlike** provider-paid comparison sites, TUCKER shows where every price came from, ranks without regard to commission, and puts a person on the phone when you want one.

Three ideas, always in this order: **We compare. We help. You choose.**

- **Mission:** make household-service decisions easy, and help people keep more of their money when a better option exists.
- **Vision:** the first place a household checks before paying another year of the same bills.
- **Emotional shift:** from confused, skeptical and put-upon to clear, confident and in control. Target reaction: *"Tucker makes this easy. I understand my options, and I don't feel pressured."*

## What customers are up against, and the brand's answer

| Barrier | Evidence | TUCKER's response |
|---|---|---|
| Hidden fees and promo price jumps | Top two internet complaints in a 2025 survey (see sources) | Show the price after the promotion and every fee before the customer asks |
| Feeling misled | 59–65% say pricing misled them | Label every number: verified, needs address check, estimate. Already in the product |
| Switching hassle and installation worry | Assumption (not measured here) | "Tucker handles the hard part": an advisor books and tracks the switch |
| Distrust of salespeople | Assumption | "You choose." No urgency language, no countdowns, commission disclosure in plain words |
| Provider loyalty, fear of a worse service | Assumption | "Keep what you have" is always an option on screen; say so when the current plan is already good |
| Bill fatigue | Majority report rising internet costs | One short check for several bills; a yearly "second opinion" habit |

Segments differ in emphasis, not in message. Renters and first-time owners: speed and simplicity. Families and established owners: total household cost. Retirees: a patient human and no pressure. No segment is addressed with stereotypes.

## Competitive read

- **Verified:** comparison sites are free to the consumer and paid by providers; at least one discloses that payment influences which providers appear. Their disclosures sit on separate pages.
- **Assumption (general knowledge, not verified this session):** the category looks and sounds alike (ZIP box, provider logo grid, blue and white, "compare providers near you"); most cover one or two categories; none found with a character mascot.
- **Openings:** (1) transparency shown on the result itself, not on a disclosure page; (2) one check for the whole household; (3) a face and a voice in a faceless category; (4) human help without a hard sell.

## Personality

Dependable, resourceful, approachable, clever, hardworking, confident, friendly, witty, protective. In one line: **the neighbor who's good with bills and doesn't make it weird.**

| Mode | Use | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday Tucker | Homepage, onboarding | "Let's see what your home is paying for." |
| Helpful Tucker | Bills, comparisons, support | "This price lasts 12 months. After that it's $85." |
| Celebrating Tucker | A real saving found, a switch completed | "Found one. That's $420 a year back in your pocket, if your address checks out." |
| Campaign Tucker | Selected ads and social only | "Your bills called. They're nervous." |

Never in any mode: guaranteed savings, invented urgency, jokes at the customer's expense, profanity in the product.

## Messaging hierarchy

1. **Name + descriptor:** tucker. Your home service savings broker. (The descriptor always accompanies the name until the brand is known; "Tucker" alone doesn't say what we do.)
2. **Promise:** Simple savings on the services your home needs.
3. **Proof:** every price labelled; ranking that ignores commission; a real person when you want one.
4. **Action:** "See what you could save" (never "Save now").

## Strategy gaps and creative risks I'd flag

1. **The cap is the only trucking cue.** No trucks, roads or CB slang in the core identity, or people will think we haul freight.
2. **The slogan works against that.** "Mother trucker" repeats the one association we need to avoid and carries implied profanity that ad platforms and older audiences may reject. Keep it to selected organic and video work, test it against a clean line ("Tucker's on it."), and don't put it in the product.
3. **A brown bear in a hat with workwear has famous neighbours.** The design must stay clear of them (territories doc §2).
4. **"Bear" has a money meaning.** A bear market is a falling one. That is usable ("we're bearish on your bills") but only as an occasional wink.
5. **Name clearance and domain are open** (decision log O2, O3).
6. **Most categories have no verified offers yet.** The brand can promise comparison today only for internet; elsewhere it promises a free review. Advertising must match that until the data exists.
