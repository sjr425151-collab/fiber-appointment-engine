# Phase 2 — Three creative territories for the Working Bear

All three are the same character: Tucker, a brown bear in a trucker-style cap and clean workwear. They differ in how he is drawn and in the identity built around him. A visual review board accompanies this document (link in the session report). **No mascot imagery exists yet**; mascot descriptions below are design intent, to be tested at Gate G1 → G2.

## 1. What stays constant (proposed Tucker constants)

- Warm cinnamon-brown fur, cream muzzle, dark nose. Stylized, never realistic fur, never plush.
- Low-crown trucker cap with a curved brim, pushed back a little so the brow shows. The front panel carries the name patch.
- A chore jacket that covers the torso, patch on the left chest. No tools, no truck.
- Closed-mouth half smile with one brow slightly raised: "let me take a look at that bill."
- Four-digit mitten paws (simpler to animate and to keep consistent).
- Hero angle is three-quarter, not straight-on.

## 2. Guardrails: distance from existing characters

| Neighbour | Their signature | Tucker avoids |
|---|---|---|
| A government-owned forest-safety bear | Campaign hat, jeans, bare chest, shovel | Any flat-brim hat, shovels, forest settings, bare torso |
| A convenience-store beaver (actively litigated lookalikes) | Red ballcap, yellow circle, wide-eyed toothy frontal grin | Red caps, yellow circular badges, buck teeth, frontal head-in-circle logos |
| Cuddly household-product bears | Pastel, soft, family-of-bears | Plush proportions, pastel palettes |
| Classic cartoon bears | Pork-pie hat with collar and tie; red hat with blue duffle coat | Neckties, bow ties, that hat and coat pairing |
| An illustrated tech-brand bear | Flat brown bear in burrowing scenes | Tunnels, burrows, similar flat proportions |

Counsel should review the chosen design against these before G3.

## 3. The territories

### A · Full Stop — Iconic Working Bear

- **Idea:** Tucker puts a full stop to bill hassle. The period in the wordmark is shaped like his nose.
- **Mascot:** flat and graphic, four or five colors, built from simple shapes. Readable at 16 px from the cap and nose alone.
- **Wordmark:** heavy, tight lowercase grotesque; a nose-shaped period.
- **Color:** white `#FFFFFF`, ink `#14110F`, spruce `#0F5A43`, honey `#F0B429` (small highlights only), cinnamon `#8B5E3C`.
- **Type:** Bricolage Grotesque for display, Inter for text, tabular figures for money.
- **Advertising:** type-led statics; short lines that end on the period. "Overpaying. Full stop."
- **Product UI:** flat, roomy, big numbers; the dot marks progress and status.
- **Motion:** quick and springy; the dot lands and settles.
- **Voice:** the shortest sentences that are still kind.

### B · Golden Hour — Cinematic Working Bear

- **Idea:** someone is looking out for your house. The light of early evening, when the bills get opened.
- **Mascot:** dimensional, with stylized materials and expressive brows, lit like an animated feature.
- **Wordmark:** soft lowercase serif with a small amber period.
- **Color:** dusk `#10262B`, porch light `#FFF4D6`, amber `#E39A2D`, denim `#40607F`, chestnut `#7A4B2E`.
- **Type:** Fraunces for display, Source Sans 3 for text.
- **Advertising:** 15–30 second films: a kitchen table at dusk, Tucker reads the bill and raises an eyebrow.
- **Product UI:** restrained interface; rendered Tucker in the hero and at milestones.
- **Motion:** slow push-ins and gentle easing.
- **Voice:** warm and unhurried.

### C · Name Patch — Balanced Working Bear

- **Idea:** the logo is the stitched name patch on Tucker's jacket. He's the one at the shop you ask for by name.
- **Mascot:** one geometry with two finishes: flat for the product, dimensional for ads. The patch appears on him in both, so the logo and the character are literally attached.
- **Wordmark:** sturdy, slightly wide lowercase inside a stitched rounded-rectangle patch; also works without the patch. The period is a square bar-tack stitch.
- **Color:** shop white `#F2F3F0`, charcoal `#22262A`, chambray `#3F6C8F`, rust thread `#B5532A`, bear `#8A5A3A`; clover `#2E7D4F` reserved for savings.
- **Type:** Archivo (wide, heavy) for display, IBM Plex Sans for text, IBM Plex Mono for bill figures, like a receipt.
- **Advertising:** "Ask for Tucker." Patches as stickers and badges; before-and-after receipts.
- **Product UI:** data-quality labels become woven labels; bill breakdowns read like receipts; stitched borders used sparingly.
- **Motion:** the patch stitches on (a drawn dashed line); confirmations stamp down.
- **Voice:** plain shop talk.

## 6. Recommendation

**Territory C · Name Patch, produced flat-first.**

- It turns the two locked elements (the cap and the workwear) into the logo system itself, so the brand is recognizable even where the bear isn't shown.
- "Ask for Tucker" positions a broker as a person you trust, without a truck in sight.
- The flat master covers the product, icons and email today; the dimensional finish is added for video once the geometry is locked. That order keeps cost and drift down.

**Tradeoffs.** C must keep two finishes in sync, and done badly it becomes neither. The fix is to lock the flat geometry first and derive the dimensional version from it. A is cheaper and sharper at small sizes but thinner in video. B has the highest ceiling for film and the highest risk of looking like a generic generated bear. A hybrid is reasonable: C's patch system with A's short-sentence headlines.

**Founder decision (2026-11-17):** Territory C with A's nose-shaped period, keeping C's typeface. The mascot itself is chosen after round-1 images are reviewed (G2).
